# The Frontiersmen — Canonical Narrative Walkthrough

## Act I — New hires

The five protagonists arrive by boat at Keldmouth expecting the ordinary humiliations of frontier employment: wet gear, suspicious officials, and guild paperwork written to make injury look voluntary. They meet at the river dock before any of them has enough information to decide whether the others are competent.

Lantern Pike hires them as one caravan-protection group. Guildmaster Halric Doss speaks of roads, villages, wolves, bog-things, and the need for discipline. He does not explain amber. At the pay table, clerk Nara Beech weighs warm gold-like lumps into their hands and answers practical questions—where it spends, how to keep it wrapped—without answering the important one.

The caravan leaves east. Its master, Maela Brigg, is experienced, unsentimental, and more concerned with reaching scheduled stops than educating new guards. On the road, a fox-shaped glimmer crosses without bending grass. Sylvi notices that no living fox follows its route. Veyra notices an answering warmth in the party's amber wages. Neither can establish causation.

At Saltmere, the party begins becoming a company. Rhea assumes responsibility without naming herself leader. Garran asks whether a monster that remembers pain can still suffer. Pip maps the camp and notices which roads Maela refuses to discuss. The exact campfire transcript later gives every character a different memory of the same first uneasy night.

## Act II — The crossroads duty

Harrowcross is not expecting a caravan. It is expecting replacement guards.

Sergeant Edrik Morn reports that his night watch was torn apart in the barracks. One survivor, Kellan Voss, remembers animal shapes moving through wood and plaster, dead companions calling in borrowed voices, and the guild-payment chest sounding as though something inside it wanted out. The party has no reason yet to accept all of his account.

Maela invokes an emergency clause in the Lantern Pike contract. The party is reassigned to Harrowcross as temporary watch and investigation detail. Then the caravan leaves. The abandonment is bureaucratically valid and emotionally brutal: the five are not asked whether they accept the new duty because their signatures already did.

They discover that Harrowcross pays the guild in amber. Before that fact can become theory, Kellan's wound reopens near the chest. The amber quickens. A wretch comes through the barracks wall, using the dead guards' voices as lures. The fight teaches the party more than the guild has told them. Veyra and Garran treat the chest as part of the battlefield; Rhea keeps the apparition away from the wounded; Sylvi tracks the way it crosses material boundaries; Pip attacks the room's mechanisms and sight lines rather than the thing's anatomy.

A second manifestation appears in the orchard. Gnome dowser Tillo Wick finally supplies an operational vocabulary: amber can be quiet, warm, or quick; blood, fear, and broken ground matter; the dowsing fork points east. Harrowcross's protection ledger shows that amber has been collected specifically to pay Lantern Pike. The same substance funding protection is implicated in the attacks.

The dowsing route leads to Saint Orra's Mine.

## Act III — Under the Frontier

Foreman Jorund Bale has sealed an upper drift. A worker is missing. The seam is warm. He refuses entry before the party asks because he knows the guild is overdue to collect amber and knows that opening the drift will make the mine responsible for whatever happens next.

The party pushes past an incomplete negotiation. This matters: the cave-in is not arbitrary punishment from the plot. It is the result of entering a live system under pressure without a full account of its risks.

Inside, an amber vein answers their presence. A wretch phases out of it. Then the drift collapses, cutting Jorund and the surface away. The exact conversation after the cave-in preserves the moment the party chooses lower air over waiting beneath unstable stone.

They travel for two days through caverns no current mine plan records. At Blackwater Crossing, the environment behaves like memory rather than geology: sounds repeat from the wrong direction; impressions remain after their causes; roots appear where no surface tree could reach. They find an old underground camp whose occupants understood enough to leave warnings but not enough to escape cleanly.

On the second night, the cavern responds when the travelers speak genuine painful memories. This creates five incompatible interpretations:

- Rhea treats the response as a threat learning command structure.
- Sylvi reads it as an ecological system disturbed by false boundaries.
- Garran believes memory has become material without becoming consent.
- Veyra sees sympathetic response and wants to test it carefully.
- Pip notices that honest statements alter routes more reliably than maps.

The Sunken Hall gives them their first ancient theory. Its tablet describes amber as golden sap through which root remembers wound. Hunter and prey return without moral distinction. The ruins do not explain whether the Heartwood Below is a god, organism, magical network, or metaphor made physically true.

They open an amber reliquary when retreat closes. The action wakes a gallery of remembered predators. The party escapes a sequence of wretches whose forms shift according to remembered pursuit—boar, wolf, antlered prey, human terror—then reaches a cold shaft and climbs to daylight.

They emerge from a hilltop sink in forest beyond every map Pip trusts.

## Act IV — The Root Host

From the hilltop they see smoke. Approaching it is dangerous, but starvation, injury, and disorientation have removed the safer options. This is an important authorial choice: the party is captured because constrained people take a rational risk, not because competent adventurers ignore obvious signs.

The camp is populated by wooden-masked people who do not speak. Their masks are individually carved and collectively terrifying. Some followers grunt, breathe rhythmically, or move in answer to drum patterns, but only the Tree King speaks doctrine.

He calls himself Aldren Veyl, once a Lantern Pike field-master. He knows the guild knot and Halric Doss. He claims the guild discovered amber's relationship to remembered slaughter at Blackroot Sink, concealed it, and converted the resulting danger into a protection economy.

Aldren's communion is not wholly fraudulent. His root crown routes impressions from amber masks into him. The party can observe genuine magical integration. His interpretation is still monstrous: he treats memory as possession, the dead as available material, and fear as a resource. He sentences the five to blood sport so the survivor will strengthen his host and the dead will strengthen it more completely.

Rootjaw, the masked champion, demonstrates how amber masks borrow remembered animal force. Moth, a silent acolyte with a moth-notched mask, never speaks. Moth's silence is preserved across the canonical transcript; the record does not manufacture a convenient whispered explanation.

Before the blood ring, the party plans with incomplete equipment and incomplete information. Moth leaves the pen open and a bone key visible. The action is deliberate but unexplained. The party escapes, recovers only essential gear, and enters the northwood.

## Act V — The hunt begins

The Root Host raises drums and pursuit calls. Rain turns the spruce dark and the ground unreliable. The party follows water where possible because Sylvi believes the masks read root and trail better than running current.

At that stage, the pursuit conversation recorded a distant boar-like bellow from Rootjaw. He is audible through the rain, but he is not physically present in the scene. That distinction became a regression test after authoring exposed how easily audible dialogue can accidentally imply scene participation.

Act V stopped at this then-live decision point before Act VI:

- Rootjaw and the host have cut south of the party.
- Pip's map no longer predicts which notches belong to old travelers and which belong to the pursuers.
- Moth's bone key has not been tested.
- Veyra still carries the reliquary.
- the party knows enough to suspect the guild but not enough to prove the entire Blackroot Compact;
- returning to Harrowcross remains both duty and danger.

Rhea's instruction is simple: keep the key, keep moving, learn what it opens when they are not being hunted.

## Act VI — Drowned Waymark

The five drop from the spruce line into flooded old roadworks at `main 196:0`,
strictly after the closed Hunt and Running intervals end at `main 195:99`.
Pip recognizes the triangular notch on Moth's bone key in a drowned warden
mark, but Rhea forbids testing the unfamiliar hooked notch.

Veyra keeps the reliquary closed and charcoal-wrapped while the party lowers
it into branching cold water. Its answering resonance divides among the
channels, sending the immediate search lines north and east. The maneuver buys
an end to this chase, not a defeat of Aldren or the Root Host.

Behind an ordinary iron survey pin, Pip finds a water-stained Blackroot route
counterfoil. It records three deaths, special rules for separating and damping
raw amber, and Halric Doss's initials. Garran and Rhea preserve the document's
edges: it corroborates concealed operational knowledge after Blackroot, but it
does not itself prove the manifestation classification or the full compact.

At `main 207:0` the party takes an abandoned warden road south. The active
conversation closes at `main 210:0` with no claim that this road reaches
Harrowcross. Rootjaw is absent from the scene and their private plan; Moth and
Jorund remain unresolved; Veyra still holds the closed reliquary; and Pip's
hooked key notch remains unused.

## Recollection divergence

The example is designed so that exact transcripts do not flatten memory.

### The Tree King's audience

The canonical transcript establishes:

- Aldren's former identity;
- knowledge of the guild knot;
- his claim that the guild wastes remembered life;
- his blood-sport sentence;
- Rootjaw's warning grunt;
- Moth's complete silence.

The party's recollections emphasize different things. Rhea remembers the doctrine as an operational threat. Garran remembers the refusal to distinguish memory from consent. Veyra remembers how the crown distributed impressions. Pip remembers the guild-specific knowledge. Sylvi remembers the forest's avoidance of the camp.

### The pursuit

The party hears Rootjaw's distant bellow, but Rootjaw hears only the later audible portion of the party's exchange. His conversation view begins at tick `193:10`; he cannot retrieve the group's earlier escape planning. Conversely, attempting to build Rootjaw a character context for **The Hunt Begins** fails because he is not physically present.

## Current author questions

The active world leaves real campaign decisions rather than merely incomplete bookkeeping:

1. Does the bone key open a Root Host cache, a mask, an older amber structure, or something Moth intends the party to destroy?
2. Can Harrowcross be protected without continuing the amber-payment compact?
3. Is Jorund alive behind the collapse?
4. How much can the party prove with the counterfoil's narrow corroboration, and what evidence is still missing?
5. What does Moth want, and is “Moth” still capable of an identity separate from the host?
6. Does the reliquary preserve knowledge, manufacture wretches, or both?
7. Can the party use amber without repeating Aldren's logic of ownership?
